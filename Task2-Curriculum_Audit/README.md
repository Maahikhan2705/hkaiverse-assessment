# Task 2 --- Curriculum Redefinition for 2030

-   **Name:** YZ. Maahi Shayada Khanam
-   **Programme:** B.Tech in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science
-   **Institution:** ICFAI Foundation for Higher Education (IFHE),
    Hyderabad
-   **Admission Batch:** 2023--2027
-   **Assessment:** HKAIVERSE × LEANOVA OPEX --- Round 2

## 1. Task Overview

This task reviews the supplied B.Tech Artificial Intelligence and Data
Science course map and proposes a practical curriculum redesign to
strengthen readiness for AI, data science, and software engineering
roles by 2030. The proposal aims to retain core mathematical and
computing foundations while making applied capability more visible
through tested code, reproducible experiments, data-quality checks,
deployment practice, and integrated projects.

The report is a proposal for review and approval; it does not claim that
the changes have already been adopted by IFHE.

## 2. Programme Identity and Evidence Basis

The supplied course map is used as the working baseline because it
contains the course codes, semester placement, and credit values
supplied for this review. The report also refers to public IFHE
programme information and a small sample of employer role pages.

The report explicitly notes that the public programme structure may not
match the exact regulation applicable to the 2023--2027 admission batch.
The sources should not be silently merged, and the official
batch-specific regulation should be checked before the credit total is
treated as the definitive graduation requirement.

## 3. Audit Method and Decision Criteria

Course titles alone do not establish the exact content, lab time, or
assessment depth. The report therefore recommends inspecting syllabi,
labs, rubrics, and student work before claiming that a capability is
absent.

The audit uses four decision types: - **KEEP:** Retain a foundational
subject and assess its outcomes clearly. - **UPDATE:** Strengthen
practical assessments within an existing course. - **REVIEW:**
Investigate possible overlap or low demand using syllabus and outcome
evidence before making changes. - **INTEGRATE:** Demonstrate
capabilities across subjects and projects where that is more appropriate
than teaching them in isolation.

The report emphasizes preserving prerequisites and teaching durable
principles alongside current tools.

## 4. Course-Level Recommendations

The course-level review proposes keeping core programming, mathematics,
data structures, algorithms, databases, systems, and AI/ML foundations.
It recommends strengthening practical assessment in areas such as: -
Code review, complexity reasoning, unit tests, and edge cases. - Data
validation, reproducible cleaning, SQL correctness, data dictionaries,
and privacy-aware handling. - Experiment design, leakage checks,
baseline comparisons, error analysis, calibration, and
reproducibility. - Retrieval grounding, abstention, prompt-injection
tests, and human review for Generative AI. - APIs, system performance,
failure handling, deployment configuration, logs, health checks, secrets
management, and rollback where appropriate. - Threat modelling, least
privilege, secure API practices, and appropriate lab safety. - Design
reviews, concise technical writing, stakeholder demonstrations, and team
retrospectives. - Reproducible project repositories, tests, evaluation,
security/ethics review, documentation, and final demonstrations.

The report does not recommend removing subjects solely because a small
sample of job listings does not mention them.

## 5. Credit-Target Redesign

The report uses **166 credits** as a working baseline derived from the
supplied course map. It proposes material changes to practical outcomes
and assessments across **39 credits** of existing courses.

The calculation is:

`39 ÷ 166 × 100 = 23.49%`, rounded to **23.5%**.

This falls within the assignment's 20--25% target on that basis. The
proposal adds no net graduation credits: changes are intended to be
embedded in existing courses and the existing Special Project, subject
to syllabus review and academic approval.

The report notes that the credit figure and affected-credit method must
be confirmed against the official 2023--2027 regulation and the
assignment's counting rules. Courses should be counted once, and
elective alternatives should be checked before treating all listed
credits as graduation requirements.

## 6. Job-Description Signals and Curriculum Mapping

A small sample of role listings is used to inform the skills emphasis.
It points to capabilities such as programming, practical machine
learning, data handling, evaluation, production-oriented systems, CI/CD,
safe deployment, and reliability.

The report treats these listings as limited, time-specific signals---not
a complete forecast of 2030 hiring and not proof that the current
curriculum lacks these skills. One senior role is used only as a
longer-term systems-engineering signal, not as an entry-level
eligibility benchmark.

## 7. Proposed AI Systems Engineering Lab / Capstone

The report proposes a "Reliable AI Workflow Engineering Lab: From
Prototype to Verified Service," preferably hosted in the existing
3-credit SP301 Special Project and integrated with Cloud Computing and
Generative AI, subject to syllabus fit and faculty approval.

The proposed exercise uses synthetic office-request records. A workflow
validates inputs, establishes a deterministic baseline, optionally uses
AI for classification or drafting, checks uncertainty, routes the result
for human approval, and logs the outcome.

Proposed deliverables include a workflow map, synthetic dataset,
repository, test log, prompt/model/date log, risk register,
demonstration, setup guide, and reflection. AI may suggest or draft, but
a person remains responsible for external messages, official record
changes, and consequential decisions.

## 8. AI-Assisted Subject Exercises and Verification

The report includes five small subject exercises: - **CS102 Data
Structures:** Identify the value whose second occurrence is reached
first. - **CS206 DBMS:** Count eligible applicants using synthetic
records, a LEFT JOIN, and conditional aggregation. - **DS304 Machine
Learning:** Demonstrate how fitting preprocessing on test data can cause
leakage. - **DS311 NLP:** Calculate classification metrics from a
confusion matrix. - **DS408 Generative AI:** Test a small FAQ workflow
with grounded answers and abstention for unsupported requests.

The report describes these as small checks against synthetic examples or
explicit correctness criteria. They do not prove that an entire subject,
AI model, or production system is error-free. If required by the
assessor, the actual tool/model label, execution date, prompts, outputs,
and verification records should be preserved rather than guessed.

## 9. Interactive Curriculum View

The report outlines a proposed interactive view with semester and
pathway filters, a status legend, credit summary, exercise cards, and a
feedback panel. This is described as a proposed feature; only include an
implemented application or working demo in the repository if one has
actually been built.

## 10. Validation, Feedback, and Implementation

The report proposes review with faculty, recent graduates or alumni,
student project evidence, and employer feedback. It does not claim that
interviews or reviews have already been completed.

Success indicators include mapped learning outcomes and rubrics,
reproducible project work, recorded AI exercise checks where required,
assigned owners and review dates for feedback, and skill mapping of
internship/capstone evidence where records are available and
participants consent.

The roadmap covers: 1. Confirm the exact batch regulation and baseline.
2. Validate the affected-credit count and method. 3. Prepare exercise
briefs, synthetic data, rubrics, tests, and project guidance. 4. Pilot
selected exercises and collect actual results and feedback. 5. Refine
the proposal and seek formal academic approval.

## 11. Limitations and Conditions for Adoption

-   The 166-credit total is a working baseline, not a verified
    definitive graduation requirement.
-   The 39 affected credits and 23.5% share depend on the counting
    method and must be reconciled with the official regulation and
    assignment rules.
-   Course changes are proposals subject to syllabus review and formal
    academic approval.
-   Employer listings are a small, time-specific sample and should be
    rechecked before relying on them.
-   Feedback, pilots, and outcomes should be reported as completed only
    after they have actually occurred.
-   Synthetic examples must not be represented as real student or
    employer data.
